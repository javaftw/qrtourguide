The Historic Stellenbosch Chair

This beautifully crafted chair dates back to the late 18th century and represents the finest craftsmanship of the Cape Dutch period. Made from indigenous yellowwood, it showcases the intricate woodworking skills that were prevalent among local artisans of that era.

The chair was originally commissioned by one of Stellenbosch's founding families and has been meticulously preserved. Notice the hand-carved details on the armrests and the unique joinery techniques used in its construction.

Historical Significance

During the colonial period, furniture like this was not only functional but also served as a status symbol. The quality of craftsmanship and choice of materials indicated the owner's social standing in the community.

This particular piece has survived numerous historical events, including the Anglo-Boer War, and continues to be an important artifact in understanding the domestic life of early Stellenbosch residents.

Conservation

The museum's conservation team has carefully maintained this piece, ensuring that it remains stable while preserving its historical integrity. The original finish has been carefully preserved, giving us insight into the materials and techniques used by craftsmen over 200 years ago.
